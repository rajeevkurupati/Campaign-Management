# HerKey Brand & Content Policy Guidelines
## Version 3.2 — Effective 1 January 2026

## 1. Platform Overview

### 1.1 About HerKey

HerKey is India's leading career engagement platform purpose-built for women professionals. Founded on the conviction that economic independence is the cornerstone of gender equality, HerKey connects millions of women across every career stage — from freshers entering the workforce to senior leaders driving boardroom decisions — with employers, learning opportunities, mentors, and a peer community.

**Mission statement:** To accelerate the economic empowerment of women in India by making careers more accessible, growth more equitable, and professional networks more inclusive.

**Platform pillars:**
- **Jobs** — Curated job listings from diversity-committed employers
- **Learning** — Courses, certifications, and skill-building programmes
- **Mentorship** — Structured 1:1 and cohort-based mentorship journeys
- **Community** — Groups, events, and peer networking spaces

### 1.2 User Demographics

HerKey serves women professionals across India and the Indian diaspora globally. The platform has users in Tier 1 cities (Bengaluru, Mumbai, Delhi NCR, Hyderabad, Pune, Chennai), Tier 2 cities (Jaipur, Lucknow, Ahmedabad, Indore, Bhopal, Kochi), and aspirational Tier 3 markets. Content and campaigns must be sensitive to this geographic, linguistic, and socioeconomic diversity.

### 1.3 Why These Guidelines Exist

Every campaign published on HerKey reflects on the platform's brand promise. A patronising campaign undermines trust. A misleading claim creates legal exposure. Content that inadvertently discriminates violates both platform policy and the spirit of the platform's mission. These guidelines exist to protect our users, our brand, and our partners.

## 2. Brand Voice Principles

HerKey campaigns must embody four voice principles: **Empowering**, **Inclusive**, **Authentic**, and **Professional yet Warm**. Each principle is defined below with explicit Do/Don't examples.

### 2.1 Empowering

The brand voice treats every woman as a capable, ambitious professional. Language should inspire action from a position of strength, not rescue from a position of weakness.

**Definition:** Messaging that recognises women's existing capability and helps them reach further — not messaging that implies they need to be "saved" or "given a chance."

**DO:**
- "Take the next step in your leadership journey."
- "Your skills are in demand. Here's who's hiring."
- "Restart your career on your own terms."
- "You've built something remarkable. Now scale it."
- "Break into the C-suite with mentors who've been there."

**DON'T:**
- "Even women can succeed in tech." *(implies default expectation of failure)*
- "We give women a chance to prove themselves." *(patronising; positions employer as benevolent gatekeepers)*
- "Don't let your career be left behind." *(fear-based, disempowering)*
- "Housewives who want to return to work can…" *(reductive identity framing)*
- "Ladies, it's time to step up." *(condescending address form)*

### 2.2 Inclusive

HerKey serves women across caste, religion, region, language, disability status, sexual orientation (to the extent they identify as women professionally), age, and economic background. Campaigns must not — even implicitly — exclude or marginalise any sub-group.

**Definition:** Language and imagery choices that make every woman feel the opportunity is for her, not just a narrow archetype.

**DO:**
- Use diverse names in hypothetical examples (not only anglicised names)
- Reference Tier 2 and Tier 3 locations when the campaign is pan-India
- Include returnees, freshers, and mid-career professionals in the same campaign where relevant
- Acknowledge that "career" includes non-linear paths

**DON'T:**
- Reference religion, caste, or region as criteria (e.g., "for women from South India")
- Use marital status as a qualifying or disqualifying attribute
- Assume all users are English-speaking or English-educated
- Imply that only young women (under 35) are employable

### 2.3 Authentic

HerKey's voice is direct and honest. Campaigns should not over-promise, use superlatives that cannot be substantiated, or claim endorsements/partnerships that do not exist.

**Definition:** Claims that are verifiable, proportionate, and honest about both opportunity and effort required.

**DO:**
- "Join 50,000+ women who have advanced their careers through HerKey." *(if the figure is accurate)*
- "Our mentors have held roles at Google, Infosys, and Deloitte." *(specific and verifiable)*
- "Average salary increase reported by bootcamp graduates: 28%." *(with methodology footnote)*

**DON'T:**
- "Guaranteed job placement in 30 days."
- "100% salary hike assured."
- "India's #1 platform for every working woman." *(unverifiable superlative)*
- "Approved by the Government of India" *(unless specific, documented approval exists)*
- Fabricate employer partnership names or logos

### 2.4 Professional yet Warm

HerKey is not a corporate newsletter, nor is it a casual social media feed. The tone should feel like advice from a trusted, senior peer — informed, direct, warm, and free of jargon.

**Definition:** Content that is grammatically correct, purposeful, and respectful without being stiff or bureaucratic.

**DO:**
- Use second-person "you" for direct address
- Use active voice: "Apply today" not "Applications are being accepted"
- Keep sentences short (under 20 words where possible in headlines)
- Use ellipsis, dashes, and lists to create breathing room in body copy

**DON'T:**
- Use excessive exclamation marks (maximum one per campaign piece)
- Use jargon without explanation ("leverage synergies", "paradigm shift")
- Use outdated formal salutations in push/email ("Dear Madam,")
- Write in a way that sounds like a government notice or HR memo

## 3. Campaign Type Definitions

HerKey supports five standard campaign types. Each type has defined required elements, success metrics, and compliance expectations.

### 3.1 Awareness Campaign

**What it is:** A campaign whose primary goal is to grow brand recognition, platform understanding, or audience education. Awareness campaigns do not always have a hard conversion CTA.

**Required elements:**
- Clear statement of what the platform/programme/event is
- A value proposition for the target audience
- A soft CTA ("Learn more", "Explore", "Follow")
- Audience segment specification

**Success metrics:** Reach, impressions, video views, profile visits, follower growth.

**Tone guidance:** Informational and inspiring. Less urgency than Hiring or Event Drive campaigns.

**Example compliant brief:** "Introduce HerKey's 2026 Returnee Programme to women who have taken a career break of 6 months or more. Explain the three programme pillars (skill refresh, mentorship, job matching) and build trust through participant testimonials. Goal: 500,000 impressions in Q1."

**Common compliance issues in Awareness campaigns:**
- Unverifiable statistics about platform scale
- Missing audience segment specification
- Overly vague objective (e.g., "make women aware" — of what, specifically?)

### 3.2 Event Drive Campaign

**What it is:** A campaign designed to drive registrations or attendance for a specific, time-bound event (summit, bootcamp, webinar, networking session).

**Required elements:**
- Event name, date, and location (physical/virtual/hybrid)
- Speaker or programme highlights
- Registration CTA with link or mechanism
- Deadline for registration
- Target registration number

**Success metrics:** Registrations, conversion rate, attendance rate, post-event NPS.

**Tone guidance:** Energetic and aspirational. Urgency is appropriate near event deadlines.

**Example compliant brief:** "Drive registrations for the Women in Tech Summit 2026 (Bengaluru, 15–17 June). Target audience: Women in Tech and Mid-Senior professionals. Featured speakers: 3 FAANG engineering leaders, 2 startup founders. Goal: 5,000 paid registrations by 31 May."

**Common compliance issues in Event Drive campaigns:**
- False urgency claims ("Only 10 spots left" when untrue)
- Missing event date or location
- Fabricated speaker credentials

### 3.3 Hiring Drive Campaign

**What it is:** A campaign connecting women professionals with open job opportunities from one or more hiring partners.

**Required elements:**
- Employer name(s) or category (e.g., "Top 25 IT employers")
- Role types or functions
- Location(s) or explicit "remote" designation
- Application CTA
- Non-discriminatory eligibility criteria

**Success metrics:** Job applications, application-to-shortlist rate, hires.

**Tone guidance:** Professional and aspirational. Clarity is paramount — candidates need to understand if the opportunity is relevant to them.

**Explicit compliance requirements:**
- No salary ranges that are gender-discriminatory
- No hidden marital status, pregnancy status, or age filters
- Must not require physical appearance criteria
- Must not contain language implying women are "preferred" in a way that suggests quota-filling rather than genuine opportunity

**Example compliant brief:** "Q2 2026 Hiring Drive for 1,000 women in software engineering, data science, and product management roles across Bengaluru, Hyderabad, and Pune. Employers: Tier 1 IT, consulting, and fintech. Target audience: Women in Tech with 2–12 years experience. Goal: 1,000 qualified applications."

### 3.4 Learning Campaign

**What it is:** A campaign promoting skill-building programmes, online courses, certifications, or structured learning journeys.

**Required elements:**
- Programme name and provider
- Skills or certification earned
- Duration and format (self-paced/live/cohort)
- Learning outcome statement
- Enrolment CTA

**Success metrics:** Enrolments, course completion rate, certification earned, post-course job/promotion rate.

**Tone guidance:** Aspirational and practical. Speak to the outcome (the career benefit) as well as the process.

**Compliance note:** Learning campaigns must not promise career outcomes they cannot substantiate (e.g., "This course will get you a job at Google"). Outcome statements should be hedged appropriately ("Many of our graduates have gone on to…").

**Example compliant brief:** "Promote HerKey's 8-week Data Analytics Bootcamp to freshers and career changers. Programme covers SQL, Python, Tableau, and storytelling. Delivered live online. Goal: 800 enrolments for the July cohort."

### 3.5 Mentorship Campaign

**What it is:** A campaign recruiting mentees and/or mentors into a structured mentorship programme.

**Required elements:**
- Programme name and cohort details
- Mentor profile description (seniority, function, industry)
- Time commitment and duration
- Application/nomination process
- Goal (number of mentee slots)

**Success metrics:** Applications, matches made, mentorship session completion rate, mentee satisfaction score.

**Tone guidance:** Aspirational and community-oriented. Mentorship campaigns should feel like an invitation, not a test.

**Example compliant brief:** "Recruit 200 mentees for Cohort 4 of the HerKey Leadership Mentorship Programme. Mentees should be mid-senior women (5–15 years experience) aspiring to leadership. Mentors are C-suite and VP-level leaders from Fortune 500 and leading Indian companies. 6-month programme, virtual. Applications close 30 April."

The following phrases have been tested and validated for resonance with HerKey's audience. Campaign copywriters should consider incorporating them.

**Career growth:**
- "Career restart" / "restart your career"
- "Return to work" / "return-to-work"
- "Upskill" / "reskill" / "future-ready skills"
- "Career pivot" / "pivot with confidence"
- "Break the glass ceiling"
- "Advance in your career"
- "From career break to career maker"
- "Own your career"

**Leadership & ambition:**
- "Women in leadership"
- "Women in tech" / "Women in STEM"
- "Lead from where you are"
- "The boardroom needs your voice"
- "Rise together"
- "Next-generation leaders"

**Community & support:**
- "Peer network" / "your tribe"
- "Mentorship that moves you"
- "Community of achievers"
- "Women who lift each other"

**Opportunity:**
- "Opportunities built for you"
- "Where ambition meets opportunity"
- "Your next chapter starts here"
- "Doors that are open for you"

### 4.2 Messaging Frameworks by Audience Segment

#### Job Seekers
**Lead with:** Specific opportunity, employer quality, ease of application
**Frame:** "Your skills are what employers are looking for right now."
**Avoid:** Urgency tactics that feel desperate; any implication of low standards

#### Freshers
**Lead with:** Learning, first opportunity, community of peers
**Frame:** "Start strong — your career begins here."
**Avoid:** Implying they are not ready; suggesting they must "prove themselves"

#### Career Break / Returnees
**Lead with:** Validation of their existing skills, structured re-entry support
**Frame:** "Your skills didn't take a break — your confidence just needs a refresh."
**Avoid:** Framing the break as a gap, deficit, or problem to be overcome

#### Women in Tech
**Lead with:** Technical roles, peer community, leadership pathways
**Frame:** "The tech world needs your expertise and your perspective."
**Avoid:** Tokenism language ("one of the few women who…"); any suggestion that being in tech is exceptional for women

#### Mid-Senior Professionals
**Lead with:** Leadership growth, executive mentorship, high-value networks
**Frame:** "You've built the expertise. Now build the influence."
**Avoid:** Suggesting they start over; condescension about experience gaps

#### Entrepreneurs
**Lead with:** Network, investor access, business-building skills, peer support
**Frame:** "Grow your business alongside women who understand the journey."
**Avoid:** Implying their entrepreneurship is a hobby; references to "women-owned" as a charitable category

#### Mentorship Seekers
**Lead with:** Access to experience, structured growth, personalised guidance
**Frame:** "Get the guidance you wish you'd had earlier."
**Avoid:** Framing mentorship as remediation; making it seem transactional

## 5. Prohibited Content Rules

The following content categories are strictly prohibited on HerKey. Campaigns containing any prohibited element will receive a compliance gate of **BLOCK** and a score below 60.

### 5.1 Discriminatory Language or Criteria

**Rule:** Campaigns must not discriminate on the basis of age, caste, religion, marital status, pregnancy or maternity status, disability, sexual orientation, or regional origin.

**Prohibited examples:**
- "Seeking women below 30 years of age" *(age discrimination)*
- "Open to unmarried women only" *(marital status discrimination)*
- "For Hindu women professionals" *(religious discrimination)*
- "Candidates from North India preferred" *(regional discrimination)*
- "Not suitable for women on maternity leave" *(pregnancy/maternity discrimination)*
- "Candidates with no physical disability required" *(disability discrimination, unless specific role requirement with legal basis)*

**Why it matters:** These formulations violate India's Equal Remuneration Act, the Maternity Benefit Act, and the spirit of the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act. They also directly contradict HerKey's mission.

### 5.2 Patronising or Condescending Tone

**Rule:** No campaign content may position women as inherently less capable, as recipients of charity, or as needing to be "allowed" access to opportunities that are theirs by right.

**Prohibited examples:**
- "Even women can excel in data science."
- "We believe women deserve a seat at the table too."
- "For housewives who want to use their free time productively."
- "Ladies, prove that you can handle a leadership role."
- "This role is suitable for women who can manage both work and family."
- "Women-friendly workplace" used as the primary selling point rather than actual role merit *(implies that not being hostile is a special benefit)*

**Assessment note:** Patronising tone is one of the most commonly encountered issues in submitted campaigns and is often unintentional. Reviewers should flag any phrase that implies women are exceptions rather than the norm in any professional context.

### 5.3 False or Misleading Claims

**Rule:** No campaign may make claims that are unverifiable, statistically unsubstantiated, or demonstrably false.

**Prohibited claims:**
- "Guaranteed job placement" *(no platform can guarantee this; prohibited)*
- "100% salary hike guaranteed" *(prohibited absolutely)*
- "Government-approved programme" *(without documented approval reference)*
- "India's #1 women's career platform" *(requires Audit Bureau of Circulations or equivalent third-party verification)*
- Specific outcome statistics ("92% of our graduates get hired") without methodology disclosure
- Fabricated employer partnership claims (listing logos of employers who have not authorised their name/logo use)
- "Approved by NASSCOM / CII / FICCI" without written authorisation from the named body

**Borderline cases:**
- "Most trusted career platform for women" — acceptable only if backed by survey data disclosed in footnote
- "Join 1 million+ women" — acceptable only if the registered user count has been verified by a third party or is clearly defined (registered users ≠ active users)

### 5.4 Off-Platform or Non-Career-Angle Content

**Rule:** Campaigns must have a genuine, discernible connection to career development, professional growth, hiring, learning, or professional networking for women. Content that does not have this connection is not suitable for HerKey, regardless of whether it targets women.

**Prohibited categories:**
- Consumer goods (cosmetics, clothing, jewellery) unless the campaign explicitly connects to a career/professional context (e.g., a webinar on building a professional wardrobe for first-generation professionals is borderline acceptable; a cosmetics ad with no career angle is not)
- Personal finance products (loans, credit cards, insurance) unless the angle is specifically around funding professional development or a women entrepreneur's business financing need
- Real estate without a career angle (a general home-buying ad is not platform-relevant; a campaign about relocation for career growth may be acceptable)
- FMCG, food and beverage, entertainment
- Political content of any kind
- Religious content of any kind

**Assessment guidance:** Reviewers should ask: "If this ad ran in a mainstream consumer magazine, would it make sense? If yes, and if it has no special career-development angle, it likely does not belong on HerKey."

### 5.5 Appearance-Based Criteria

**Rule:** No campaign may mention physical appearance, body type, skin colour, height, weight, or physical attractiveness as relevant to professional opportunity.

**Prohibited examples:**
- "We're looking for presentable candidates." *(vague but implies appearance criteria)*
- "Candidate must have a smart, professional appearance." *(acceptable in a grooming standards context for specific roles such as client-facing hospitality, but must be stated neutrally and job-relevantly; default is prohibited)*
- Any reference to complexion, height, or weight in hiring criteria
- Before/after transformations framed around appearance rather than confidence or skills

### 5.6 Content Implying Women Are Less Capable

**Rule:** No content may — even subtly — suggest that women underperform men, that women need special accommodations because of inherent limitations, or that women's professional participation is conditional on circumstances (e.g., "if your husband supports you").

**Prohibited examples:**
- "Women are naturally more collaborative, making them ideal for support roles." *(gender essentialism that limits ambition)*
- "This programme is perfect for women who want to work without too much pressure." *(assumes women cannot handle challenge)*
- "Even with limited technical background, women can succeed in this bootcamp." *(implies low expectations)*
- "Designed for women who want flexible work because of family responsibilities." *(reduces women's career motivation to family constraint)*
- "Help women break their fear of public speaking." *(public speaking fear is not gender-specific; framing it as a women's problem is patronising)*

## 6. Audience Segment Profiles

### 6.1 Job Seekers (Active)

**Who they are:** Women currently looking for a job, whether employed (looking to switch) or unemployed. Range from 1 year to 20+ years experience. High intent, outcome-focused.

**Platform behaviour:** Frequent job board visitors, high email open rates, high responsiveness to push notifications. Make decisions relatively quickly.

**What resonates:**
- Specific job listings with clear role, employer, and location
- Salary range transparency
- "Apply in 2 minutes" or other friction-reduction messaging
- Company culture signals around inclusion and growth
- Success stories from women in similar roles

**What to avoid:**
- Generic motivational content with no clear action
- Requirements-heavy language before the value proposition
- Any hint that the opportunity is limited by personal circumstances

**Scoring note:** Hiring Drive campaigns targeted at this segment should score 85+ if employer is credible, role is clearly described, and there are no discriminatory criteria.

### 6.2 Freshers (0–2 years experience)

**Who they are:** Recent graduates or early-career women, often navigating their first professional environment. High anxiety about "fitting in"; strong peer influence.

**Platform behaviour:** Community-oriented, engage heavily with peer content, learning content, and event content. More price-sensitive than other segments.

**What resonates:**
- Community language ("you're not alone", "join 10,000 freshers who...")
- Low-barrier entry points (free courses, free events)
- Clear outcome statements (what skill/credential they will earn)
- Role models close to their age/stage

**What to avoid:**
- Implying they need to prove themselves before they are welcome
- Language that assumes prior professional experience
- Expensive paid offerings without a free trial or scholarship mention

### 6.3 Career Break / Returnees

**Who they are:** Women who have taken a break from paid employment for any reason — childcare, eldercare, personal health, relocation, or choice — and are re-entering or considering re-entry. Break length varies from 6 months to 10+ years.

**Platform behaviour:** Lower session frequency than active job seekers; high content engagement. Need trust-building before conversion. Often need permission (psychological) to re-enter.

**What resonates:**
- Validation of existing skills ("your skills didn't expire")
- Structured, low-risk re-entry pathways
- Community of other returnees
- Employer partners who specifically welcome returnees
- Honest timeline setting ("Most returnees find a role within 3–6 months of structured preparation")

**What to avoid:**
- Framing the career break as a "gap" or "problem" — it is a fact, not a failing
- Implying they need to start from scratch
- False urgency ("jobs disappearing fast, apply now")
- Any reference to why they took the break (never assume childcare; never mention "domestic" or "household")

**Scoring note:** This is one of HerKey's highest-empathy audience segments. Campaigns that unintentionally stigmatise the career break (even with good intent) should be flagged with an 'error' severity issue.

### 6.4 Women in Tech

**Who they are:** Women working in software engineering, data science, product management, UX design, cybersecurity, DevOps, ML/AI, or adjacent technical functions. Typically 2–15 years experience. High professional identity.

**Platform behaviour:** Engage with technical learning content, company-culture content, and leadership pathways. Selective about general women's empowerment content that feels too broad.

**What resonates:**
- Technical specificity (name the stack, the framework, the function)
- Peer community at the same technical level
- Leadership narratives from senior women in their specific field
- Companies with documented gender pay equity and senior women representation data

**What to avoid:**
- Overly generic "women can code too" messaging *(they already code — they don't need this validated)*
- Emphasis on "women-friendly" as the headline benefit *(leading with culture over role is patronising for senior candidates)*
- Implying technical roles are exceptional achievements for women

### 6.5 Mid-Senior Professionals (5–15 years experience)

**Who they are:** Managers, senior individual contributors, directors, VPs. Established careers. Looking for advancement, executive coaching, high-value networks, and premium opportunities.

**Platform behaviour:** Lower overall platform time but high content quality bar. Email-responsive. Event-driven (attend premium, low-volume events). Mentorship programme applicants.

**What resonates:**
- Exclusivity and selectivity ("cohort of 50", "by application only")
- Peer-level executives as mentors, not junior coaches
- Measurable career advancement outcomes
- Brand-name employers and institutions

**What to avoid:**
- Generic empowerment messaging they have heard many times
- High time commitment without clear ROI
- Career basics content positioned at them

### 6.6 Entrepreneurs / Business Owners

**Who they are:** Women who run their own businesses, from solo consultants to founders with funded startups. Often use HerKey for peer community, investor introductions, and B2B visibility.

**Platform behaviour:** Community and event-oriented. High engagement with peer founder content. Selective about learning (specific, advanced business content only).

**What resonates:**
- Peer founder community and networks
- Investor and funding access
- Business growth skills (scaling, finance, go-to-market)
- Recognition and visibility platforms

**What to avoid:**
- Treating entrepreneurship as a fallback from employment
- Micro-enterprise-only framing when the segment includes tech founders
- Any implication that their business is "small" or "side hustle" in nature

### 6.7 Mentorship Seekers

**Who they are:** Women at any stage who are specifically looking for structured guidance from a more senior professional. Motivation is typically career acceleration, navigating specific challenges, or executive transition.

**Platform behaviour:** Programme-oriented; complete onboarding and commitment steps that others abandon. High NPS contributors.

**What resonates:**
- Programme structure and credibility of mentor pool
- Outcomes from previous cohort participants
- Application process that signals quality and selectivity
- Community of peer mentees

**What to avoid:**
- Framing mentorship as remediation ("if you're struggling")
- Vague mentor credentials
- Open-ended, unstructured offers

## 7. Scoring Rubric

### 7.1 Score Bands

| Band | Score | Gate | Meaning |
|------|-------|------|---------|
| Excellent | 90–100 | PASS | Campaign is fully compliant, clearly relevant, empowering, and legally sound. No revisions required. |
| Good | 80–89 | PASS | Minor tone or clarity issues that are acceptable but would benefit from revision. Campaign may be published. |
| Needs Revision | 60–79 | BLOCK | At least one issue that must be addressed before publishing. Campaign cannot go live without changes. |
| Rejected | 0–59 | BLOCK | Fundamentally unsuitable. Either off-platform content, serious compliance violation, or multiple major issues. |

### 7.2 What Takes a Campaign from 60 to 80

A campaign scoring 60–79 typically has one of the following issues:

**Issue A — Vague campaign objective:**
A brief that says "we want women to know about our programme" without specifying what action is sought, what the programme does, or who it is for. Revision: add specific CTA, target audience, and conversion goal.

**Issue B — Unsubstantiated claim (minor):**
A brief that says "thousands of women have benefited" without specifying numbers, or uses "leading" without clarification. Revision: replace with verifiable figure or remove superlative.

**Issue C — Borderline tone:**
A brief that uses a phrase like "help women get back on track" — not grossly patronising, but framing the career as "off track" is subtly negative. Revision: reframe as "support women in accelerating their next career chapter."

**Issue D — Missing required element:**
A Hiring Drive brief that lists employer name and function but omits location. Revision: add location or explicit "remote" designation.

### 7.3 What Takes a Campaign from 80 to 90

A campaign scoring 80–89 typically:
- Has a clear objective and audience
- Contains no prohibited content
- May have a slightly generic or flat headline that does not leverage HerKey's brand voice
- May have a body copy that is accurate but not compelling
- May lack specificity in the audience segment (e.g., "women professionals" rather than "women in data science with 3–8 years experience")

To move from 80 to 90: sharpen the headline to be more specific and action-oriented, tighten the body copy to the most compelling 2–3 sentences, and ensure the audience segment is precisely defined.

### 7.4 What Takes a Campaign from 90 to 100

A 90–100 campaign:
- Has a compelling, specific, on-brand headline
- Clear, substantiated body copy with specific numbers or outcomes
- Precisely defined audience segment with geography
- Measurable, time-bound objective
- Uses power phrases from the approved language library
- Has no tone, compliance, or factual issues

### 7.5 Borderline Case Examples

**Case 1: Career break returnee programme with one vague sentence**
Brief: "Join our programme for women returning to work. We provide training and support. Register by 30 April."
Assessment: The brief is relevant and non-discriminatory. However, "training and support" is vague — what training? What support? The objective is missing (how many registrations?). Score: 72 / BLOCK. Issues: (1) brief — warning — describe the specific training components; (2) objective — warning — add target registration count.

**Case 2: Hiring drive with minor off-tone headline**
Headline: "Ladies, It's Time to Show What You Can Do"
Assessment: "Ladies" as an address form is borderline — not grossly offensive, but dated and slightly condescending in a professional context. "Show what you can do" implies the women haven't been showing their capability yet — a subtle disempowerment. Score: 74 / BLOCK. Issue: headline — error — replace "Ladies" with direct second-person ("Ready to make your next move?") and remove implication of unproven capability.

**Case 3: Tech bootcamp with false outcome claim**
Body copy: "Guaranteed placement in a tech role within 60 days of completion."
Assessment: "Guaranteed placement" is a prohibited false claim regardless of context. Score: 45 / BLOCK. Issue: body_copy — error — remove guarantee language; replace with supported outcome statement.

**Case 4: Mentorship programme with correct content but wrong campaign type**
Brief says "Hiring Drive" but content is entirely about a mentorship programme.
Assessment: Misclassified campaign type is a clarity issue. Score: 68 / BLOCK. Issue: overall — error — campaign type should be "Mentorship", not "Hiring Drive"; metrics and required elements differ.

**Case 5: Awareness campaign with no clear audience**
Brief: "Raise awareness about HerKey among working women."
Assessment: Too broad to be measurable or actionable. Which women? In which geography? Through which channels? Score: 62 / BLOCK. Issues: (1) audience — warning — specify segment; (2) objective — warning — define measurable awareness metric.

**Case 6: Women in Tech hiring drive — fully compliant**
Brief: "Hiring drive for senior software engineers (5–12 years) at 3 FAANG-equivalent companies. Roles in Bengaluru and Hyderabad. Open to all women with backend engineering experience. Goal: 500 applications in 6 weeks."
Assessment: Specific, non-discriminatory, clear objective, relevant to platform. Score: 95 / PASS.

## 8. Platform-Specific Compliance Checks

### 8.1 ASCI (Advertising Standards Council of India) Guidelines

All campaigns published on HerKey are subject to ASCI's Code for Self-Regulation of Advertising Content in India. Key applicable provisions:

**Chapter 1 — Truthfulness and Honesty:**
- Advertisements must not contain statements or visual presentations that directly or by implication, omission, ambiguity, or exaggerated claim are likely to mislead the consumer.
- Comparative claims must be factually accurate and capable of substantiation.
- Testimonials must reflect genuine consumer experience.

**Chapter 3 — Non-offensive to society:**
- Advertisements must not deride any race, caste, colour, creed, or nationality.
- Advertisements must not present women in an indecorous, derogatory, or demeaning manner.

**Chapter 5 — Fair in competition:**
- Campaigns must not denigrate competitors or their products.

**HerKey-specific ASCI notes:**
- Campaigns with statistics must include a source footnote (e.g., "Source: HerKey User Survey, Q4 2025, n=12,000")
- Testimonials used in campaigns must have written consent from the individual
- "India's leading / #1 / most trusted" claims must have documented, third-party substantiation filed with HerKey's legal team before campaign launch

### 8.2 Employment Advertisement Regulations in India

Hiring Drive campaigns are subject to the following:

**Equal Remuneration Act, 1976:**
- No campaign may specify different pay scales or different eligibility criteria for the same role based on gender (while HerKey targets women professionals, the role itself must be genuinely open and not a reduced opportunity)

**Maternity Benefit Act, 1961 (as amended 2017):**
- No hiring campaign may state or imply that women on maternity leave, women who have recently returned from maternity leave, or pregnant women are ineligible or less preferred
- Eligibility criteria must not screen for pregnancy status

**The Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016:**
- Unless the physical capability is a documented, essential job function, campaigns must not specify physical requirements that would exclude persons with disabilities
- "Able-bodied" as a criterion is prohibited without specific, documented job necessity

**Information Technology Act, 2000 (as amended):**
- Campaigns collected personal data (e.g., "register here") must have a compliant privacy notice
- Campaigns must not promise data privacy that the platform cannot guarantee

### 8.3 HerKey Platform Terms of Service — Key Points for Campaign Creators

The following points from HerKey's Terms of Service are directly relevant to campaign compliance:

1. **Employer verification:** Hiring Drive campaigns may only feature employers who have completed HerKey's employer verification process. Unverified employers may not be named in campaigns.

2. **Salary claim review:** Any campaign that mentions specific salary figures (range or absolute) must have those figures reviewed by HerKey's employer partnerships team before publication.

3. **Programme accreditation:** Learning campaigns that mention external certifications (e.g., "AWS Certified", "Google Analytics Certified") must confirm that the programme is an officially authorised pathway to that certification.

4. **Event capacity:** Event Drive campaigns must accurately reflect the available capacity. "Limited seats available" is permitted only if fewer than 25% of total capacity remains.

5. **Image and copy rights:** Campaign creative assets (images, copy) must be licensed for commercial use. Use of stock imagery must comply with the licensing terms.

6. **Disclosure of sponsored content:** If a campaign is paid for by a third-party employer or learning partner, it must be disclosed with a "Sponsored" or "In partnership with [Name]" label visible without scrolling.

### 8.4 HerKey Community Standards for Campaign Content

Beyond legal compliance, all campaigns must conform to HerKey's community standards:

- **No external social media linking** that takes users off-platform to a competitor's property without explicit approval from HerKey's partnerships team
- **No competitor mentions** unless in a factual, non-disparaging context approved by HerKey's legal team
- **No unsolicited messaging promises** ("We will contact you by WhatsApp") without user consent to the specific contact channel
- **No user data collection** via campaign forms that is outside the scope disclosed in HerKey's Privacy Policy

## Appendix B: Glossary

**Career Break:** A voluntary or involuntary period away from paid employment. HerKey does not distinguish between reasons for a career break and does not treat it as a disqualifying factor.

**Campaign Brief:** The primary description of the campaign's purpose, target audience, and desired outcome, submitted by the campaign creator.

**Gate:** The binary compliance decision — PASS (score ≥ 80, no errors) or BLOCK.

**Power Phrase:** A pre-approved phrase from the HerKey approved language library that has been tested for resonance with the target audience.

**Returnee:** A woman who is returning to paid employment after a career break. HerKey uses this term neutrally and respectfully.

**Scoring Rubric:** The framework used to translate qualitative compliance findings into a numerical score from 0 to 100.

**ASCI:** Advertising Standards Council of India — the self-regulatory industry body whose code governs advertising standards in India.
